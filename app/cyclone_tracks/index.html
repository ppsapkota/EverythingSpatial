Methodology

How this map is made

An exploratory prototype tool.

Tracks come from IBTrACS v04r01 (NOAA NCEI) — the merged global best-track archive that combines the world's tropical-cyclone warning centres into one dataset. The main layer covers seasons 2015–2026; every line segment is one best-track fix, and provisional (pre-reanalysis) recent seasons are kept and flagged.

Intensity is coloured on the Saffir–Simpson scale from the US agency values (USA_SSHS / USA_WIND) — 1-minute sustained wind in knots. Those are used because they are globally consistent; agencies report over different wind-averaging periods, so values are never mixed. Year and month are read from each fix's timestamp, not the meteorological season label.

The year, month, basin, name and intensity filters work per storm: if any part of a track meets a filter, the whole track is shown. So choosing Category 5 draws the complete path of every storm that reached Cat 5 at any point — including its weaker genesis and decay stages.

The El Niño layers isolate the strong and very-strong episodes since 1980 (ranked by NOAA CPC's Oceanic Niño Index). Each shows storms that reached Category 3+ during that episode's two seasons, drawn as complete tracks; the monthly chart tallies those storms for whichever episode layers you enable.

Key terms

Track fixes

Count of best-track position records for a storm — one per ~6-hourly time step. This is the "Track fixes" figure in the storm card.

Category

Saffir–Simpson class from USA_SSHS, based on 1-min sustained wind. TD/TS are pre-hurricane stages.

Landfall

First fix flagged LANDFALL = 0 — the storm centre reaches land within the next 6 hours.

Provisional

Recent seasons are published before reanalysis and may be revised. Flagged on the storm card.

Sources: IBTrACS v04r01, NOAA NCEI · El Niño ranking: NOAA CPC Oceanic Niño Index (ONI) · Basemaps: Esri, CARTO, OpenStreetMap. Geometries are cut at the 180° meridian so Pacific tracks don't wrap across the map.
